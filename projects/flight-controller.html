The board

This is the flight controller for the start-up's drone: the board that reads the motion sensors and runs the flight software. It's built around an STM32H743 microcontroller in a 169-ball BGA package with a 0.5 mm ball pitch, with a BMI323 inertial measurement unit, a BMM350 magnetometer, a BME280 pressure sensor, a 16 MHz crystal and a USB-C connector.

What I did

I owned the PCB layout. The circuit itself came from the company's schematic engineer, and I worked alongside them to turn it into a board that could be made.

  • Checked every footprint against the manufacturer's drawing.
  • Ran the electrical and design rule checks (ERC and DRC) and resolved every violation.
  • Where the choice of part was mine, compared the options on performance, size and cost.
  • Kept the board's interfaces compatible with what the mechanical and electronics engineers were building around it.
  • Dealt with suppliers on procurement.
  • Delivered the manufacture-ready files: Gerbers, drill files, the bill of materials and pick-and-place data.

Inside the board

The board has six copper layers in an impedance-controlled stack-up. Most of the parts and short routes sit on the top layer, inner layer 2 is a solid copper plane, and the other inner layers carry most of the signal routing. 257 blind vias run from the top layer down to one of the inner layers, the usual way to escape-route a 0.5 mm-pitch BGA, alongside 13 through-holes and 4 slots.

Also at Atreides: repairing ESCs

A batch of electronic speed controllers had burnt tracks and excess solder. I worked out that the damage came from earlier rework rather than failure in service, so the units could be saved. I desoldered the damaged areas, cleaned them with isopropyl alcohol, bridged the burnt tracks with wire links and checked continuity before returning them to spares.

What I took from it

Joining a start-up this early showed me how a small company actually works: everyone pitches in and takes on far more than their job title. Working directly with the CEO and seeing what he does day to day was the most valuable part.
