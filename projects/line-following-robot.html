Line-following robot

A Year 3 team design project: a robot that follows a line, detects obstacles and makes its own decisions. I lead the six-person team and own the firmware.

Team
Six students (Team 7), Team Design Project 3
My role
Project lead and firmware
When
Oct 2026 to now
Microcontroller
NXP KL25Z (Arm Cortex-M0+, 48 MHz) on a FRDM-KL25Z board
Motor driver
L298 dual H-bridge
Status
Firmware builds; first drive at the interim demo by 20 Nov 2026

Leading the team

I planned the project on a Gantt chart and gave each stage a named lead: schematic, breadboard prototype, PCB design and build, software and final assembly. I chose the L298 dual H-bridge motor driver and split the electronics into three boards: power distribution (12 V, 5 V from a 7805 and 3.3 V from an LD1117), motor drive, and a breakout for the microcontroller.

While writing the wiring guide I noticed that the planned breakout left no free pins for the line sensors, and asked for the board's analogue header to be broken out before the PCB was made.

Firmware

The firmware is register-level C with no operating system or Mbed layer, so every peripheral is set up directly from the reference manual.

  • Motor control with 10 kHz PWM, ramping from stop to full speed over 250 ms, a 100 ms stop before reversing (the gearmotor's datasheet asks for it) and braking at zero speed.
  • A 1 ms SysTick timebase for timing, and UART debug output at 115200 baud.
  • Calibration and motor-test modes, and the interim route stored as a table of distances and turns.

Fail-safes

  • If the processor faults, the fault handler cuts both motors.
  • 10 kΩ pull-down resistors hold the motor enables low while the microcontroller is in reset or being programmed, so the wheels can't start on their own.

Tooling for the team

I wrote a Python tool that installs the compiler, builds the firmware, flashes the board through its USB drive, checks that programming succeeded and opens a serial monitor, all run from VS Code tasks, so anyone in the team can build and flash with one command.

The firmware builds but hasn't driven the robot yet, and its calibration values are placeholders. I'll update this page after the interim demo.
