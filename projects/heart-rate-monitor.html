Heart rate monitor

A Year 2 team project: a battery-powered monitor that reads the pulse from a finger clip and shows the heart rate in beats per minute. Four of us built it as KEFF Tech (Team 21).

Team
Four students, Team 21 (KEFF Tech)
Course
Electronic Design Project 2, University of Glasgow (2025 to 2026)
My part
Circuit, PCB layout, build and code, worked on with the team; unofficial team lead until February 2026
Microcontroller
NXP LPC1768 on an mbed board
Tools
OrCAD Capture, Allegro, Mbed, oscilloscope

How it works

A clip on the fingertip shines infrared light into the finger, and a phototransistor on the other side picks up the small changes in light as blood pulses through. This is photoplethysmography, or PPG. The raw signal is tiny and sits on a large steady offset, so a two-stage band-pass amplifier built around MCP6292 op-amps strips the offset and amplifies the pulse, running from a single 3.3 V rail with a 1.65 V reference.

An LPC1768 microcontroller samples the pulse, smooths it, and counts a beat each time the signal rises through a threshold, ignoring anything too soon after the last beat. Beats per minute come from the time between beats. The reading appears on an LCD, an 8×8 LED matrix driven by a MAX7221 shows a beating heart or the pulse trace, and an RGB LED flashes with each beat. A PP3 9 V battery and an LM2937 regulator power the board.

Results

On the finished board, a resting reading of 58 beats per minute rose to 88 after a walk outside, and a smartwatch gave similar readings.

My part

We didn't split the work into fixed roles: all four of us worked on the circuit, the PCB layout in Allegro, building and testing, and the microcontroller code. Until I left for my exchange semester in Chengdu in February 2026, I acted as the team's unofficial lead, and I set up our risk register.

The life cycle assessment, the final report and presentation, and the photos and videos were done by the rest of the team after I left, so none of them are shown here.
